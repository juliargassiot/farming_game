# Data

`grass.json` drives the painted ground, grass and stone paths alike: `dials` shape the patches (size, how much of the ground is light or dark, how far edges bleed, how many sprigs) the paths' stones (`stone_size`, grain, edge light and shade), and the pebbles on dirt and rock, `pack` says which sprigs to cut from the grass pack sheets, and `seasons` give each season's palette of three grass tones, its `stone` mortar and shades, its `rock` ledge and `dirt` trail shades, its `cliff` colours, and the sheets it draws from. Edit it and run `tools/art/grass_paint.py` to repaint `assets/grass/`; judge the result in the world previews.

`maps/world.txt` is the overworld, one character per tile (legend in `scripts/world/world_map.gd`); beside every map, a `.json` of the same name names its districts (race, kind, tile rectangle) and anchors its buildings. `maps/fangridge.txt` is the mountain, generated by `tools/art/mountain.py`. `tools/world_overview.py [map]` draws both into `previews/<map>-overview.png`, and `tools/screenshot.sh scenes/world/world.tscn <name> <x,y> [map]` renders any spot at play scale.

`mountain.json` shapes the mountain map: the peak, how far apart its terraces step, the noise that roughens them, wall height and crumbling, the apron, boulder rates, snow line, the trails as waypoint curves, and where its homes (each noting whether it will be built in a tree or the rock) and trees stand. `tools/art/mountain.py` writes `maps/fangridge.txt` and its `.json` from it (run it after editing), and the ground painter draws the same geometry.

`palette.json` holds each district's colours; a map's `.json` names the palette its painting and props are snapped to.

`trees.json` is one entry per tree kind: the prop it draws, whether it chops for a wood (each race's wood feeds its own furniture later) or shakes for fruit, and the driftwood that washes up on the coast instead.
